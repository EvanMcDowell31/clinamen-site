## Who it’s for: Clinics, and the people they train

Anatomy is a graph already: a muscle hangs between two bones, takes orders from a nerve, gets blood from an artery, and moves a joint that other muscles move too. Here you can follow those ties.

- **Physios and trainers** · Show a client the muscle and the exercises for it, then send them home with a handout.
- **People who train** · What does this lift hit, and what does this stretch reach? Pick an exercise and the body shows what it works.
- **Students** · Physio, massage, nursing, medicine and personal training students learn each muscle’s origin, insertion, nerve and action. Here, that table is a graph you can walk.

## How it works: Point, follow, look, hand over

1. **Point or ask.** Tap the body, or type a muscle, a nickname (“lats”, “traps”), a lift (“rdl”, “bench”) or a movement in plain words (“bend the elbow”). It names what you mean.
2. **Follow the ties.** Click any dot and the graph turns around it: from the biceps to its nerve, to the spinal root C5, to every muscle C5 reaches. Back and forward work as in a browser.
3. **Look in 3D.** Turn the body, zoom in and peel it layer by layer: skin, muscles, heart and vessels, nerves and brain, organs, bones. Tap any of its 2,723 parts to name it.
4. **Hand it over.** Add the muscles to work on and the exercises to do, with a dose and a note for each. Then print the handout, or send it as a link.

## For physios and trainers: A handout your client can take home

Gather what you showed in the session: the muscles to work on, the exercises and stretches, a dose and a note for each. Your client gets one page: the body lit where the work is, the muscles in plain words, and each exercise with its steps.

- **On paper.** Print it, or save it as a PDF.
- **As a link.** The handout travels inside the link itself, after the #, the part a browser never sends to a server. Your client opens it on their phone and sees only their handout.
- **No client data kept.** Nothing is stored anywhere but the link and your own browser, and the client’s name never goes into the link.

## Questions: What people ask first

**Does it diagnose pain?** No. It is for learning, not diagnosis: it names the parts of the body and how they connect, and never says what is wrong with anyone. For pain, see a professional.

**Where does my client’s information go?** Nowhere but the link and your browser. A handout sent as a link carries itself after the #, which a browser never sends to a server, and the client’s name never goes into it. A handout you are still putting together stays in your own browser.

**Do I need an account, or to install anything?** No. It is one web page that runs in the browser on a phone or a computer, with no sign-in. Once it has loaded, it works without a connection.

**Where do the facts come from?** From Wikipedia’s anatomy articles. Each card links the revision its facts came from, so you can check them there.

**What will it cost?** The price isn’t set yet. The plan is a price per seat for clinics; until then, the prototype is open to try.
